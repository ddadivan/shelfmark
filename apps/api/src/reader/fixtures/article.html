Slow bread at home

Share this post

Good bread needs time more than it needs skill. A long, cool rise lets the flour develop flavour, the crumb open up and the crust turn deep brown in the oven, and it asks almost nothing of you except patience and a little planning the evening before.

Start with a simple dough: flour, water, salt and a small amount of yeast. Mix it until no dry flour is left, cover the bowl and leave it on the counter for an hour, then fold it a few times so the gluten gets stronger without any kneading at all.

The overnight rise

Put the covered bowl in the fridge and forget about it until morning. The cold slows the yeast down, which gives the bacteria in the dough time to make the acids that taste like a bakery loaf. Twelve to eighteen hours is a good window for most kitchens.

  • 500 g bread flour
  • 375 g cool water
  • 10 g salt
  • 2 g instant yeast

Time is the one ingredient you cannot buy,

but it costs nothing.

Baking

Heat the oven with a heavy pot inside for at least half an hour. Tip the dough onto a floured counter, shape it gently into a round, drop it into the hot pot and bake it covered for twenty minutes, then uncovered until the crust is as dark as you like. 

bake(covered: 20 min, 250 °C)
bake(open: 25 min, 230 °C)

Let the loaf cool on a rack for an hour before you cut it, because the crumb is still setting while it is warm. It keeps for two days in a paper bag, and stale slices make wonderful toast, croutons or a thick, comforting bread soup in winter.
