Why I stopped using a to-do app

A quiet year with paper

Sample Writer 6 min read Jun 2, 2026

For years I kept my tasks in an app. It had tags, smart lists, reminders and a streak counter that made me feel guilty on weekends. Last January I deleted it and bought a plain notebook, mostly as an experiment.

The first week was clumsy. I forgot a dentist appointment, and I wrote the same task on three pages. But something else happened: because writing is slow, I only wrote down what I really meant to do. The list got short, and a short list is a list you can actually finish.

What changed

Three things stood out after a few months of using the notebook every morning and every evening.

  • I planned fewer things, and finished more of them.
  • Reviewing the week meant turning real pages, which felt like a small ritual.
  • I stopped tuning the system and started doing the work.

The best system is the one you do not have to maintain.

If you want to try it, keep the rules small. One page per day, a [ ] box for each task, and a line at the bottom for tomorrow. That is all the method there is, and it fits on a sticky note.

[ ] write the report
[ ] call the dentist
[x] buy stamps

I still use a calendar for meetings, because other people need to see it. For everything that is only mine, paper is enough, and I do not miss the notifications at all.
